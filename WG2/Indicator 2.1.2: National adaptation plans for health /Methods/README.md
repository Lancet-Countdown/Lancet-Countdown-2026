## Methods

Progress on **Health National Adaptation Plans (HNAPs)** is monitored through the **WHO Health and Climate Change Global Survey**. Data on member state or territory membership in the **Alliance for Transformative Action on Climate Change and Health (ATACH)** are used for comparisons.

### ATACH Commitments

Commitment 1 of the COP26 Health Programme calls on member countries to develop a **HNAP informed by a Vulnerability and Adaptation (V&A) assessment**, as part of their broader National Adaptation Plan.

ATACH membership reported in this indicator was determined in **September 2025**, covering **104 member countries/areas across six WHO regions**:

- Africa: **31**
- Eastern Mediterranean: **15**
- Europe: **17**
- Americas: **20**
- South-East Asia: **7**
- Western Pacific: **14**

Further information on ATACH is available here:

- [ATACH Commitments](https://www.who.int/initiatives/alliance-for-transformative-action-on-climate-and-health/commitments)

### WHO Health and Climate Change Global Survey

The **2025 WHO Health and Climate Change Global Survey** provides complementary data across WHO member states.

There are **194 member states and territories**. As of September 2025, there were **146 responses** to the survey, including **145 WHO member states and the occupied Palestinian territory**, which is a member of ATACH but not a WHO member state.

Unlike the ATACH tracker, the survey captures whether countries have **ever completed a HNAP**, regardless of when it was developed. It is therefore a cumulative measure rather than one restricted to the post-2020 period.

Survey responses are self-reported by **Ministry of Health focal points**, often following a multistakeholder consultation process.

Further information on the WHO Health and Climate Change Global Survey is available here:

- [WHO Monitoring Science and Evidence on Climate Change and Health](https://www.who.int/activities/monitoring-science-and-evidence-on-climate-change-and-health)

### Stratification

Results are stratified by **Human Development Index (HDI)** category using classifications from the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) Human Development Reports.

The four HDI categories are:

- Low
- Medium
- High
- Very high

This enables equity-informed analysis of disparities in national adaptation planning capacity.

Results are also presented by **WHO region**.

## Caveats and Limitations

Several important limitations apply.

The WHO Health and Climate Change Global Survey relies on **self-reported information** and does not systematically capture the comprehensiveness, quality, or degree of implementation of completed HNAPs.

ATACH membership is voluntary, and participating countries may differ systematically from non-members in their institutional capacity and political will to develop adaptation plans.

Finally, as with all national-level indicators, aggregate figures may conceal substantial **subnational variability** in planning coverage and implementation, particularly in large or administratively decentralised countries.
