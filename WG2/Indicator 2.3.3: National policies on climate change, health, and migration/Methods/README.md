# Methods

This indicator tracks national-level policies that address migration, displacement, relocation or immobility specifically in relation to **climate change**, and whether these policies also mention **health or well-being**.

The indicator reports:

1. The number of currently valid national-level policies covering migrants, migration, displacement, displaced people, relocation, relocated people or immobility in relation to climate change.
2. The number of these policies that also mention health or well-being, together with a qualitative analysis of how health and/or well-being are addressed.
3. The number of countries with at least one such policy.
4. The number of these countries whose policies mention health or well-being, together with a qualitative discussion of how these issues are addressed.

## Definition of a country and eligible policy

For this indicator, a **country** is defined as a sovereign state or a significantly autonomous non-sovereign territory with substantial jurisdiction over climate change, migration and health policy.

Multilateral, intergovernmental and international policies are excluded.

For a policy to be included, there must be an explicit reference to:

- **climate change**
- migration, mobility, displacement, relocation or immobility

For the health component, there must also be an explicit reference to:

- **health**
- **well-being**

Broader or related terms such as “climate”, “climate disasters”, “humanitarian” or “environmental” are not treated as equivalent to an explicit reference to climate change.

## Identification of policies

National-level policies were identified using two approaches:

1. a systematic review using the keywords that define the indicator
2. crowd-sourcing and expert queries

Because it is not possible to know whether all relevant policies have been identified, the reported values should be interpreted as **minimum counts**.

Policies that are no longer valid are retained in a separate list. A further list records documents that were considered but did not meet the inclusion criteria.

## Policy classification

Each included policy was categorised according to the type and direction of mobility addressed.

### Direction of mobility

Policies were classified as addressing:

- migration, mobility, displacement or relocation **from a location**
- migration, mobility, displacement or relocation **to a location**
- **immobility or trapped populations**

### Domestic and international mobility

Policies were also classified as addressing:

- **domestic** migration, mobility, displacement or relocation
- **international** migration, mobility, displacement or relocation

Immobility is classified as domestic by definition.

A single policy can be counted in more than one category where it addresses several forms or directions of movement.

Policies both with and without a specified end date are included if they remain valid.

## Qualitative analysis

The document analysis is qualitative.

Specific statements linking climate change, migration and health are compared with the scientific evidence available before the policy was published.

The indicator therefore assesses not only whether climate change, migration and health are mentioned together, but also how these relationships are presented within national policy.


# Caveats and Limitations

The main challenge in developing an indicator linking climate change, migration and health is attribution.

It is difficult to determine whether migration, displacement, relocation or immobility can be attributed directly to climate change. It is also difficult to attribute health outcomes directly to these forms of mobility.

The scientific literature continues to debate the nature and strength of relationships between:

- climate change and migration, displacement, mobility, immobility or relocation
- health and well-being and migration, displacement, mobility, immobility or relocation

This indicator partly avoids these attribution problems by focusing on **what national policies state**, rather than attempting to establish whether the underlying causal relationships are correct.

A policy either explicitly links climate change with migration-related issues or it does not. Similarly, a policy either explicitly discusses health or well-being or it does not.

Where policies make questionable or unsupported links between these issues, the qualitative analysis can examine those claims against the available scientific evidence.

The indicator therefore reflects how national governments recognise and frame connections between climate change, migration and health, rather than measuring the actual causal relationships between them.

The indicator is intentionally simple and does not aim to provide a complete or comprehensive assessment of all climate-related mobility or health processes.

Another important limitation is language coverage. Most of the documents identified are in English, although documents in some other languages are included. Policies that are not available in English may sometimes be identified through English-language academic publications, news articles or other sources, but searches in languages other than English are not systematic or complete.

As a result, the reported number of policies should be interpreted as a **minimum number of policies meeting the inclusion criteria**. It is possible to count the policies that were identified, but it is not possible to determine how many relevant policies may have been missed.

The number of countries included can also change slightly between years. These changes are generally small, but they can alter the baseline used for comparison.

Finally, historical counts can change when a previously unidentified but still-valid policy from an earlier year is discovered and subsequently added to the dataset.
