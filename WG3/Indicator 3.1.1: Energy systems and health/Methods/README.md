# Indicator 3.1.1: Energy Systems and Health

Indicator 3.1.1 tracks changes in the energy system that are relevant to climate change, air pollution, and human health.

The indicator includes three components:

1. **3.1.1.1 Carbon Intensity of the Energy System**
2. **3.1.1.2 Coal Phase-Out**
3. **3.1.1.3 Zero-Carbon Emission Electricity**

---

# 3.1.1.1 Carbon Intensity of the Energy System

## Metrics

Two metrics are included:

1. **Global CO₂ emissions from energy combustion by fuel**
   - Unit: **GtCO₂**
   - Period: **1990–2023**

2. **Carbon intensity of the energy system**
   - Unit: **tCO₂/TJ**
   - Available at global and regional scales
   - Period: **1990–2023**

## Definition

Carbon intensity represents the tonnes of CO₂ emitted for each unit of total energy supplied:

> **Carbon intensity = CO₂ emissions from fossil fuel combustion / Total Energy Supply (TES)**

Total Energy Supply (TES) represents the total amount of primary energy used in a country, taking account of energy production, imports, and exports.

Higher carbon-intensity values indicate a more fossil-fuel-dominated energy system and generally greater reliance on high-carbon fuels such as coal.

Reductions in the carbon intensity of the energy system can therefore indicate progress towards climate mitigation and may also deliver health benefits through reductions in air pollution.

## Geographic Groupings

Results can be presented by:

- Country
- WHO region
- Human Development Index (HDI) group
- Global aggregate

## Data Coverage

The underlying dataset covers most countries worldwide from 1960 to 2023, with provisional data available for selected countries for 2024.

For this indicator, the analysis uses:

**1990–2023**

## Data Source

**International Energy Agency (IEA)**  
*Greenhouse Gas Emissions from Energy*  
(previously *CO₂ Emissions from Fuel Combustion*)

The dataset is accessed through the IEA website for institutional subscribers.

## Updates Introduced in 2026

The underlying IEA dataset was updated to include data through 2023.

IEA datasets are updated periodically, with broader geographical coverage added during successive releases. Historical values may also be revised when new editions of the dataset are released.

As a result, values reported for previous years may differ slightly from those presented in earlier Lancet Countdown reports.

## Caveats and Limitations

IEA estimates are based on a combination of:

- Data supplied by national governments
- Statistical estimation
- Energy-system modelling

The quality and completeness of estimates can vary between countries and energy commodities.

Further methodological information is available in the IEA *Greenhouse Gas Emissions from Energy* documentation.

# 3.1.1.2 Coal Phase-Out

## Metrics

Two main metrics are included:

1. **Total primary energy coal supply**
   - Unit: **EJ**
   - Available by country and region

2. **Electricity generation from coal**
   - Absolute generation: **TWh**
   - Share of total electricity generation: **%**

## Definition

The indicator tracks changes in coal use because coal remains an important source of both greenhouse gas emissions and air pollution.

Total primary coal supply is calculated by aggregating the following IEA coal categories:

- Anthracite
- Coking coal
- Lignite
- Other bituminous coal
- Sub-bituminous coal

The share of electricity generation from coal is calculated as:

> **Coal electricity share (%) = Electricity generated from coal / Total electricity generation × 100**

Tracking both total coal supply and coal-fired electricity generation provides information on the role of coal within national and regional energy systems.

Declining coal use is expected to contribute to climate mitigation and improvements in air quality.

## Geographic Groupings

Results can be presented by:

- Country
- WHO region
- HDI group
- Global aggregate

## Data Coverage

The data cover approximately **156 countries**.

For this indicator, the analysis uses:

**1990–2023**

Data before 1990 are not included because of incomplete time series for several countries.

## Data Source

**International Energy Agency (IEA)**  
*World Energy Balances – Extended Energy Balances*

The dataset is accessed through the IEA website for institutional subscribers.

Full data are available through 2023.

## Updates Introduced in 2026

The underlying IEA dataset was updated to include data through 2023.

Provisional 2024 data are available for selected countries and energy products but are not included in this analysis.

Historical values may be revised by the IEA when the dataset is updated. Therefore, estimates for previous years may differ from those shown in earlier Lancet Countdown reports.

## Caveats and Limitations

IEA energy data are produced using a combination of:

- National government submissions
- Statistical estimation
- Modelling

Data quality and completeness may vary by country and energy commodity.

Further methodological details are available in the IEA *World Energy Balances* documentation.

# 3.1.1.3 Zero-Carbon Emission Electricity

## Metrics

Two electricity-generation metrics are included, with each presented in both absolute and relative terms.

### Modern Renewable Electricity Generation

- Absolute generation: **TWh**
- Share of total electricity generation: **%**

### Low-Carbon Electricity Generation

- Absolute generation: **TWh**
- Share of total electricity generation: **%**

## Modern Renewable Electricity Definition

Modern renewable electricity generation includes the following IEA categories:

- Geothermal
- Solar photovoltaics
- Solar thermal
- Tide, wave and ocean
- Wind

> **Modern renewables do not include hydro or nuclear electricity generation in this indicator.**

## Low-Carbon Electricity Definition

Low-carbon electricity generation includes:

- Nuclear
- Hydro
- Geothermal
- Solar photovoltaics
- Solar thermal
- Tide, wave and ocean
- Wind

The low-carbon metric therefore includes both renewable technologies and nuclear electricity generation.

## Calculation

Absolute electricity-generation metrics are calculated by aggregating gross electricity generation from the relevant technologies.

The corresponding electricity shares are calculated as:

> **Technology share (%) = Electricity generated from selected technologies / Total electricity generation × 100**

## Rationale

Increasing electricity generation from renewable and other low-carbon technologies can reduce reliance on fossil-fuel-based electricity generation.

Replacing fossil-fuel generation, particularly coal, can reduce greenhouse gas emissions and air pollution, providing benefits for both climate and health.

Generation is used rather than installed capacity because actual electricity generation reflects the amount of fossil-fuel-based electricity that can potentially be displaced.

## Geographic Groupings

Results can be presented by:

- Country
- WHO region
- HDI group
- Global aggregate

## Data Coverage

The underlying dataset covers approximately **156 countries** from 1971 to 2023.

For this indicator, the analysis uses:

**1990–2023**

Earlier years are excluded because of incomplete data for several countries.

## Data Source

**International Energy Agency (IEA)**  
*World Energy Balances – Extended Energy Balances*

The dataset is accessed through the IEA website for institutional subscribers.

Full data are available through 2023.

## Updates Introduced in 2026

The underlying IEA dataset was updated to include data through 2023.

Provisional 2024 values are available for selected countries and products but are not included in this analysis.

Historical data may also be revised when new IEA editions are released. Consequently, estimates for previous years may not exactly match those presented in earlier Lancet Countdown reports.

## Caveats and Limitations

IEA data are based on a combination of:

- National government reporting
- Statistical estimation
- Modelling

Coverage and data quality may vary across countries, years, and energy technologies.

Further methodological information is available in the IEA *World Energy Balances* documentation.
