# Methods

Current dietary changes are associated not only with substantial environmental impacts, but also with a high health burden of diet-related diseases. To inform the co-benefits of dietary changes towards healthier and more sustainable diets, we quantified the current burden of diet-related diseases by conducting a **comparative risk assessment** for dietary risk factors.

Below, we describe the estimation of dietary intake and the comparative risk assessments based on those dietary exposures.

## Baseline Consumption Data

We estimated baseline food consumption by adopting estimates of food availability from the **Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) food balance sheets** and adjusting these for:

- Food wasted at the point of consumption
- Energy requirements needed to sustain measured levels of body weight, height, and physical activity

This proxy for food consumption was disaggregated by age and sex by adopting the same age- and sex-specific trends as observed in dietary surveys.

 For full details of the methodology, see **[Methods 3.3.2](./Methods%203.3.2.docx)**.
