# Methods

Indicator 3.5.2 assesses the extent to which key actors in the health community are working to **measure, disclose and manage health sector greenhouse gas emissions**.

The indicator covers three groups:

- National governments
- Scientific community
- Healthcare industry

Measurement is treated as a prerequisite for effective decarbonisation. Without robust and transparent emissions data, it is difficult to identify major sources of emissions, design effective mitigation strategies or track progress over time.

Together, the three sub-components act as proxy measures of **effort towards health system decarbonisation**:

- governments establish baseline assessments and strategic direction
- the scientific community generates reliable product-level carbon data
- industry provides public commitments and transparent emissions reporting

Across all three sub-components, effort is classified as:

- **Established** — all relevant quality criteria are met
- **Progressing** — some criteria are met, or relevant work has been announced but not yet published
- **Limited** — there is limited or no evidence of high-quality measurement or effort

# National Governments

This sub-component evaluates the development of high-quality **national health system carbon footprint assessments**.

Assessments are included where they are:

- published by a national health ministry
- commissioned by a national health ministry
- formally endorsed by a national health ministry
- explicitly referenced as a baseline in a government health-sector mitigation strategy, such as a low-carbon transition plan

Assessments are evaluated against WHO guidance on measuring greenhouse gas emissions in health systems.

Four appraisal criteria are used to assess whether studies have sufficiently comprehensive measurement boundaries and whether governments have committed to maintaining and updating their assessments.

Assessments meeting all criteria are classified as **Established**.

Assessments meeting only some criteria, or where an assessment is reported to be underway but has not yet been published, are classified as **Progressing**.

## National Government Appraisal Criteria

### Published and Comprehensive Scope

#### Organisational Boundary

The organisational boundary should:

- be clearly defined
- cover all major components of the national healthcare system
- be consistent with WHO guidance

The organisational boundary should be defined using comprehensive health-system expenditure data, such as:

- National Health Accounts
- financial ledger data from a national health service, ministry, agency or provider
- other national accounts of health-system expenditure
- multi-regional input-output modelling that captures total health-system expenditure

The criterion is not met where:

- the study covers only a sample or subset of healthcare organisations
- a sample of healthcare organisations is scaled up to estimate whole-system emissions without using a comprehensive expenditure dataset

#### Operational Boundary

The operational boundary should be clearly defined and include:

- all upstream supply-chain emissions, including international sources
- all Kyoto Protocol greenhouse gases
- desflurane, sevoflurane and isoflurane where these are material

The criterion is not met where major sources of emissions are excluded.

For example, a study reporting only fuel and electricity-related emissions without purchased goods and services would not meet the criterion.

#### Base Year

The assessment year or emissions base year must be clearly defined.

### Base Year Review

The most recent national health-system carbon footprint should have been published or updated within the previous **five years**.

This reflects the expectation that base-year emissions assessments are periodically reviewed and updated.

# Scientific Community

This sub-component evaluates the availability of **carbon footprint evidence for health-related products used in healthcare systems**.

Decision-ready product-level greenhouse gas emission estimates are identified from the **Lancet MedZero database**.

The database compiles healthcare product carbon footprints from multiple international sources.

Each entry undergoes a quality review covering:

- transparency
- definition of system boundaries
- consistency of functional units
- appropriateness of life-cycle inventory data sources
- replicability of inventory data
- allocation methods

Additional candidate studies and data sources identified in the wider scientific literature but not yet formally reviewed are classified as **Progressing**.

Validated studies are mapped individually to relevant **United Nations Standard Products and Services Code (UNSPSC)** commodity codes.

The UNSPSC system provides a structured taxonomy of goods and services used across procurement systems and is used here to represent the universe of healthcare products.

This sub-component evaluates:

- the number of decision-ready carbon footprint estimates available
- the number of healthcare commodity categories covered by these estimates
- the remaining relevant commodities for which only more limited carbon footprint evidence is available

Coverage is calculated as the proportion of relevant **medical products and pharmaceuticals** for which at least one decision-ready carbon footprint estimate is available.

# Healthcare Industry

The healthcare industry sub-component evaluates public emissions disclosure, targets and governance arrangements that enable companies to quantify and manage their emissions.

The analysis assesses sustainability commitments and reporting by healthcare companies included in the **2025 Forbes Global 2000**.

Corporate commitments are evaluated using a subset of transparency and quality criteria designed to assess greenhouse gas emissions commitments and adapted for relevance to the healthcare sector.

Each company receives a binary **yes/no** assessment for each criterion.

Companies must satisfy **all four criteria** to be classified as having credible climate commitments.

The Forbes Global 2000 is used as a consistent and internationally comparable denominator for major publicly listed healthcare companies.

A total of **106 healthcare companies** were screened for public sustainability or other climate-related reporting.

## Healthcare Industry Appraisal Criteria

| Criterion | Description |
|---|---|
| **Published and comprehensive scope** | The company measures all Kyoto Protocol greenhouse gas emissions across its entire value chain, including international upstream supply-chain emissions. The measurement boundary should be aligned, at minimum, with WHO guidance for health-system carbon footprinting. |
| **Public emissions reporting to corporate standards** | The company publishes a complete emissions inventory at least once every two years and follows a recognised reporting or monitoring standard, such as the International Sustainability Standards Board, Global Reporting Initiative, Carbon Disclosure Project, UN Global Compact or Science Based Targets initiative. |
| **Net zero target by 2050** | The company has a public emissions reduction target aligned with the ambition of the Paris Agreement, with net zero across all emissions sources by 2050 at the latest. This requires at least 90% absolute reductions from the base year, with carbon credits or offsets used only for residual emissions that cannot be eliminated. |
| **Interim emissions targets** | The company has a quantified interim greenhouse gas emissions reduction target covering all emissions scopes for a defined year or multi-year period between 2025 and 2035. |

# Data

The indicator uses the following data sources:

- **National government assessments:** national health ministry climate and sustainability reports identified through the WHO Alliance for Transformative Action on Climate and Health database, web searches, PubMed and Scopus
- **Scientific community:** Lancet MedZero database
- **Healthcare product classification:** UNSPSC commodity codes covering medical products and pharmaceuticals
- **Healthcare industry:** corporate sustainability reports, financial accounts and other climate disclosure documents from healthcare companies included in the 2025 Forbes Global 2000
- Healthcare companies are identified from the Forbes categories **Health Care Equipment & Services** and **Drugs and Biotechnology**

Because emissions reporting is often delayed, all relevant documents published by **22 May 2026** were included to maximise coverage of information relating to 2025.

# Caveats and Limitations

This indicator measures progress in the healthcare community's ability to **quantify and report emissions**, rather than reductions in emissions themselves.

High-quality emissions measurement and reporting are necessary for effective decarbonisation, but they do not guarantee that mitigation measures will be implemented or that emissions will decline.

The indicator therefore reflects the **effort and measurement capability required to support decarbonisation**, rather than the eventual outcomes of those efforts.

The indicator also relies on publicly available information.

Undisclosed decarbonisation activities cannot therefore be captured.

Public transparency remains important because disclosure and commitments support:

- accountability
- benchmarking
- comparison across organisations
- knowledge sharing

However, the existence of detailed sustainability documents or public commitments does not necessarily indicate that meaningful emissions reductions are taking place.

Governments and companies may publish extensive sustainability information without implementing corresponding mitigation actions.

To reduce this limitation, the appraisal criteria focus specifically on:

- transparency
- data quality
- comprehensive measurement
- regular reporting
- time-bound emissions targets

Over time, a lack of substantive action should become increasingly visible through the measurement and reporting systems monitored by this indicator.
