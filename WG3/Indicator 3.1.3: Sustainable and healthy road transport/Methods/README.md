## Methods

Road fuel use data, by fuel type, from the **IEA World Extended Energy Balances** are divided by corresponding population statistics from the **United Nations, Department of Economic and Social Affairs, Population Division**.

### Fuel Groupings

The fuel flows from the IEA are combined as follows:

- `Biofuels` = Biodiesels + Biogasoline + Biogases + `Other liquid biofuels`

- `Fossil fuels` = `Natural gas liquids` + `Natural gas` + `Motor gasoline excl. biofuels` + `Liquefied petroleum gases (LPG)` + `Refinery gas` + `White spirit & SBP` + `Kerosene type jet fuel excl. biofuels` + `Gas/diesel oil excl. biofuels` + Lubricants + Naphtha + `Fuel oil` + `Other kerosene` + `Other oil products` + Bitumen

- `Electricity` is given by the existing IEA total.

Totals for a given year and country are divided by the corresponding country population and then summed to produce the final estimate.

This approach avoids including the populations of countries that are not covered by the IEA dataset.

- **UN World Population Prospects, 2024 edition.**

## Caveats and Limitations

IEA data are generated using both direct input from national governments and modelling. As a result, the data are subject to caveats that vary by energy commodity and country.

Full details are provided in the **IEA World Energy Balances documentation, July 2025 edition**. This documentation also describes changes to methodology introduced in previous editions of the IEA World Energy Balances.

This indicator captures changes in **total fuel use and the type of fuel used for transport**, but it does not capture shifts in modes of transport.

In particular, it does not capture **walking and cycling for short trips**, which can provide substantial health benefits through increased physical activity.

In addition to pollutants generated through fossil fuel combustion, tyre wear is estimated to account for **3–7% of airborne PM2.5 particulates worldwide**.
