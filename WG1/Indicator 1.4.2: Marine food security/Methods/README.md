# Methods

Sixteen major marine basins and five inland water basins, defined using FAO Fishing Areas, were selected because of their importance in terms of projected climate change impacts and vulnerabilities. The remaining three selected basins are located in Antarctica.

A total of **173 countries and territories** located within these basins were included to assess climate-related changes in Sea Surface Temperature (SST), deterioration of major coral reef sites, and changes in per capita consumption of capture-based fish.

## Sea Surface Temperature

A total of **1,331,304 observations**, defined by geographic position and time, were used to estimate variations in SST.

The average SST for each country was calculated as the mean SST across all coordinates within the country's coastal waters.

Regional SST estimates were calculated using two approaches:

1. **Weighted regional average:** country-level SST values were weighted according to the number of coordinates available for each country.
2. **Unweighted regional average:** all country-level SST estimates were given equal weight regardless of the number of coordinates available.

Measured SST data were obtained from the **Ocean Reanalysis System 5 (ORAS5)** and covered **1958–2025**.

Projected SST data were available for **2015–2100**, with annual values presented for **2040–2060**.

## Coral reef thermal stress

Coral reef thermal stress was assessed using data from **NOAA Coral Reef Watch**.

The analysis used the **annual maximum Bleaching Alert Area caused by thermal stress**, presented at five-year intervals between **1985 and 2025**.

## Seafood production and consumption

Capture-based and farmed-based per capita fish consumption was assessed for the countries included in the analysis using data covering **1950–2023**.

FAO seafood production statistics were used as a proxy for seafood consumption because comprehensive consumption data were not available across all countries.

Global per capita seafood consumption was approximated by dividing total global seafood production by the corresponding world population.

## Health impacts

Health impacts were assessed using estimates from the **Global Burden of Disease (GBD)** study.

The analysis extracted disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) for cardiovascular diseases, for all ages and both sexes, attributable to the risk factor:

> **Diet low in seafood omega-3 fatty acids**

The GBD estimates cover **1990–2023**.

Within the GBD framework, these estimates are based on exposure to the dietary risk factor and the relative risks associated with relevant health outcomes, compared with the **Theoretical Minimum Risk Exposure Level (TMREL)**.

# Data

The indicator uses the following data sources:

- **Coral reef thermal stress:** annual maximum Bleaching Alert Area caused by thermal stress from NOAA Coral Reef Watch.
- **Health impacts:** DALYs from cardiovascular diseases attributable to a diet low in seafood omega-3 fatty acids from the Global Burden of Disease 2023, covering 1990–2023.
- **Seafood production:** capture-based and farmed-based seafood production from FAO FishStatJ, Global Fishery and Aquaculture Production Statistics, covering 1950–2023.
- **Measured SST:** Ocean Reanalysis System 5 (ORAS5), covering 1958–2025.
- **Projected SST:** CMIP6 projections under the SSP1-2.6 and SSP3-7.0 scenarios.

# Caveats and Limitations

The Global Burden of Disease estimates linking low seafood omega-3 intake with cardiovascular diseases do not distinguish between the type or source of seafood consumed.

This is important because capture-based and farmed fish can differ in their omega-3 fatty acid content.

Fish production data are used as a proxy for fish consumption. This does not provide a direct measure of consumption, but a comprehensive alternative dataset covering all countries included in the analysis is currently unavailable.
