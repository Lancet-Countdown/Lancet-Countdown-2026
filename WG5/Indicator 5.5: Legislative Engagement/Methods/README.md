This folder contains the data sources, caveats and methods for the Legislative Engagement indicator.

## Data

The data source for this indicator is the **Climate Change Laws of the World (CCLW) legislative dataset**. The version used for this indicator was updated on **4 March 2026**.

The CCLW database is updated on a rolling basis through several structured processes. First, country profiles are revised through in-depth research reviews of national policy responses, including systematic assessment of legislation referenced in countries' most recent **Biennial Transparency Reports (BTRs)** and **Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs)** submitted to the UNFCCC, alongside additional relevant laws identified during this process.

Second, the database is supplemented annually through the integration of relevant legislative and policy documents from external sources, including the **NewClimate Institute's Climate Policy Database**, the **UN PreventionWeb platform**, and the **Gold Standard Carbon Market Regulations Tracker**.

Third, targeted thematic reviews conducted by the CCLW team—such as recent work on adaptation policies and environmental impact assessment (EIA) regimes in Latin America and Asia—contribute further documents identified through focused research activities.

## Caveats and Limitations

1. **Database updates and reproducibility**

   The CCLW database is updated monthly, meaning that datasets downloaded at different times may vary. To ensure reproducibility and transparency, the specific version used in this study has been archived and made available on GitHub.

2. **Document availability and database maintenance**

   The database operates under the assumption that all listed laws remain active unless otherwise indicated. However, links are not systematically reviewed, which can result in outdated or broken links (e.g., 404 errors).

   In the version used for this indicator, **3 documents could not be accessed** for this reason, and **10 records did not contain a URL** that could be used for text extraction.

   Additionally, documents are incorporated by the Grantham Research Institute on a largely ad hoc basis, which may affect the consistency and completeness of the dataset.

4. **Interpretation of keyword-based classifications**

   The keyword-based annotation pipeline assigns binary classifications; it does not measure the substantive depth, ambition, or implementation effectiveness of health provisions within legislation.

5. **Scope of legislative instruments**

   The focus on formal legislative instruments means that important governance activity occurring through sub-national legislation, administrative regulations, or non-legally-binding policy frameworks is not captured.

6. **Small states and territories**

   Country-level data should be interpreted with caution for small states and territories with low overall legislative volumes, where a single law can significantly affect indicator values.

7. **Loss and Damage legislation**

   **Loss and Damage** as a legislative category has only recently emerged internationally, particularly following COP27 in 2022. Legislative counts in this category therefore reflect its relatively recent emergence rather than limited ambition per se.

8. **European Union legislation**

   EU legislative documents are documented separately from national-level country data due to the EU's supranational institutional structure and shared competencies with Member States.

   EU-level acts may require transposition into national legislation, creating a risk of double counting if they are simultaneously attributed to individual countries. Furthermore, EU legislative documents can trigger more localised legislative responses within individual Member States.

   To avoid duplication, EU documents are treated as a distinct data stream, reported separately (e.g., in a dedicated EU sheet), and included only in aggregated global counts. This approach ensures conceptual clarity, prevents duplication, and maintains consistency in attribution across countries and regions.
