# Methods

This indicator measures **public engagement with health and climate change using Google Trends**.

Google Trends provides information on online search behaviour through the Google search engine. The analysis focuses on online information-seeking behaviour as one dimension of public engagement.

The indicator covers the period from **January 2014 to December 2025**, using monthly data.

Google Trends provides search-query data that reflect patterns of search activity across countries and over time.

Google does not provide raw search volumes. Instead, it reports a **normalised index of search interest**, ranging from **0 to 100**.

A value of:

- **100** represents the point of highest search popularity for the selected search term, location and period
- **50** represents search interest that is half as high as the peak
- **0** represents very low search activity rather than necessarily indicating that no searches occurred

Two approaches are used to collect and analyse Google Trends data.

## Search Term Approach

The first approach uses specific search terms.

Google Trends is provided with a defined search term, and its popularity is analysed over time and across countries.

For the main analysis, the combined search term:

**“health climate change”**

is used to identify searches containing both health and climate change concepts.

The combined search term is analysed in three widely used languages:

- English
- Spanish
- French

Results are presented separately for each language.

The analysis also compares searches for **“climate change health”** with other search terms relating to different climate change impacts or health issues.

Geographical variation in searches for this combined term is also analysed for **2025**, the final year included in the analysis.

## Topics and Categories Approach

The second approach uses **Google Trends topics and categories**.

Unlike the search-term approach, which identifies searches explicitly containing health and climate change terms, this approach can capture searches for climate change that do not directly mention health but are associated with health-related subject areas.

Topics represent groups of search terms that refer to the same underlying concept across multiple languages. Google generates these classifications using automated natural-language-processing methods.

Google Trends provides a topic for **climate change**, but does not provide a specific topic for **climate change and health**.

The climate change topic is therefore combined with broader Google Trends categories.

The indicator extracts searches for the **climate change topic** within:

- Public Health
- Health

This approach captures searches relating to climate change within a health context across multiple languages.

## Google Trends Normalisation

Google does not make raw search volumes publicly available.

Instead, Google Trends provides a normalised search-interest index based on the proportion of searches containing the term of interest relative to total search activity for a defined population and time period.

Let \(x_i\) represent the proportion of searches for a particular search term, such as **“climate change health”**, relative to total search volume within a specific geographic and temporal unit, such as a country-month.

The normalised index is calculated as:

```math
\text{index}_i
=
\frac{x_i-\min(x)}
{\max(x)-\min(x)}
```

This transformation scales the search ratios between **0 and 1**.

Google Trends subsequently presents search interest on its standard **0–100 scale**.

Google does not disclose the underlying maximum and minimum search proportions used in the normalisation or the total number of searches per user in each country.

The indicator therefore relies on the normalised search-interest data provided by Google Trends.

# Data

The indicator uses:

- **Google Trends data:** [Google Trends](https://trends.google.com/trends/)

The analysis uses monthly Google Trends data covering **January 2014 to December 2025**.

# Caveats and Limitations

Google Trends captures a specific dimension of public engagement: **online information-seeking behaviour**.

This may differ from other forms of public engagement, including:

- activism
- political participation
- voting
- offline information-seeking
- other forms of behavioural engagement

Google does not make raw search volumes publicly available, so the indicator relies on the normalised search-interest index provided by Google Trends.

Internet access and use also vary substantially between countries.

Internet use is generally higher in high-income countries than in low- and middle-income countries. This may introduce geographic differences in the representativeness of Google search activity.

Google Trends also excludes searches generated by very small numbers of users to protect privacy and prevent unstable trends.

As a result, search terms with very low search volumes may not appear in the dataset and may instead be recorded as **0**.
